Clinical Exposure

Understand the day-to-day reality of surgical practice through specialty introductions, reflective learning, and structured exposure. Clinical access depends on host authorization, supervision, patient consent, and local requirements.

  • Specialty introductions and resident perspectives
  • Theatre or clinic shadowing where authorized
  • Structured reflection on clinical experiences

Surgical preparation includes how you communicate, work with others, teach, and take responsibility. Develop these habits through educational contributions and clearly defined roles.

Research & Academic Development

Move from reading evidence to asking a useful question and contributing to a well-supervised project. Research integrity, appropriate ethics review, and fair authorship remain central throughout.

Participation

A few things
to know.

How can I express interest?

Email JSPI with your stage of training and the area you would like to explore. Individual activities have their own participation arrangements; an expression of interest does not reserve a place.

Who can contribute as an educator or mentor?

We welcome enquiries from residents, surgeons, academic educators, and experienced peer teachers. The appropriate role depends on the activity, expertise, and supervision requirements.

How is clinical exposure arranged?

Any clinical activity requires authorization from the host institution and appropriate supervision, patient consent, and local permissions. Clinical access is arranged for specific activities rather than granted through website participation.

Can an institution help deliver an opportunity?

Yes. Educational speakers, supervised workshops, venues, research supervision, mentorship, and program evaluation are all useful areas for a collaboration conversation.
