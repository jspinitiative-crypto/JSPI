The educational rationale

Connect the pieces
of career preparation.

Students need a way to connect what they learn with the practical decisions involved in pursuing a surgical career.

JSPI responds to the fragmentation that can arise between career guidance, skills development, research participation, and mentorship. This is the initiative’s educational rationale; local needs assessment and participant feedback guide how the program develops.

The intended beneficiaries are medical students, interns, and residents. Educators and institutions gain a common framework for defining activities, setting learning objectives, and understanding participant development.

A coherent educational model

A common starting point.
Progressive engagement.

The flagship format provides consistency. The wider pathway allows activities to respond to learner needs and institutional capacity.

01 / Entry point

The Surgical Day

A recurring educational series with one lecture in each of four domains: Knowledge, Technical Skills, Research, and Future of Surgery. Topics vary between editions.

02 / Development

The wider pathway

Four developmental stages connect learning with practical opportunities. Clinical exposure, professionalism and leadership, and wellbeing extend the framework beyond the flagship event.

03 / Reflection

Evidence of progress

The Surgical Passport and points framework organize verified learning and contributions. Evaluation considers learning, engagement, inclusion, quality, and continuity.

Ways to collaborate

Contribute where
your expertise matters.

A collaboration can begin with a specific educational contribution. Scope, responsibilities, supervision, resources, and evaluation are agreed for the activity.

Educational speakers

Contribute a domain lecture, a specialty perspective, a career discussion, or a research methods session with clear learning objectives.

Skills & simulation

Support practical workshops with appropriate tutors, equipment, facilities, assessment checklists, and participant-to-tutor ratios.

Mentorship & career guidance

Share experience through structured mentoring, resident perspectives, portfolio discussions, and application or interview preparation.

Research supervision

Help learners develop focused questions, sound methods, ethical research processes, and transparent academic contributions.

Venues & learning resources

Support access to teaching spaces, simulation facilities, materials, and resources appropriate to the activity and its participants.

Program evaluation

Help design needs assessments, learning measures, participant feedback, and improvement cycles that inform future delivery.

Governance and delivery

Clear roles.
Accountable delivery.

The organizational framework separates educational advice, program coordination, track delivery, and local activity support. Advisory review supports educational quality; activity leads coordinate content, logistics, and evaluation.

Skills, research, careers, mentorship, communications, collaboration, and wellbeing each have defined responsibilities within the framework. Clinical activities require host approval, and research activity follows relevant ethics and supervision processes.

Documentation, continuity between cohorts, and transparent contribution records support sustainable delivery.

Evaluation and learning outcomes

Measure what
helps learners progress.

Baseline assessment, activity records, skills checks, and participant feedback inform program improvement. First-year benchmarks guide resourcing and are reviewed against delivery capacity.

Evaluation should consider participation across training stages and institutions, practical barriers to access, and the quality of the learning experience. Residency outcomes are contextual follow-up measures; individual selection depends on the relevant training programs and authorities.

The development roadmap

Build the foundations.
Grow through delivery.

The first-year sequence connects organizational preparation with educational delivery and review. Timing follows resources, permissions, and activity readiness.

Months 1–2

Foundation

Establish educational oversight and delivery roles, assess learner needs, and prepare the tools for participant records and activity evaluation.

Month 3

Launch

Introduce the framework through Surgical Day, share participation arrangements, and begin focused career guidance resources.

Months 4–6

Build

Develop foundational skills and research learning, mentoring groups, and structured study or career conversations.

Months 7–9

Grow

Extend skills and specialty exploration, support academic collaboration, and develop application preparation for relevant learners.

Months 10–12

Review & showcase

Bring learning and academic work together, evaluate delivery, document outcomes, and prepare the next cycle.
