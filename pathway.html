These stages are guides rather than mandatory academic classifications or residency requirements. Year ranges are indicative and vary between training systems.

01

Early medical school · typically years 1–2

Explorer

Discover surgery. Build your foundations.

Start with curiosity. Learn what surgical work involves, become comfortable with foundational concepts, and explore specialties before narrowing your interests.

  • Join a Surgical Day and explore the four domains.
  • Develop applied anatomy and sterile-technique foundations.
  • Begin suturing practice in an appropriate learning setting.
  • Learn research basics and how to approach a supervisor.
02

Middle medical school · typically years 3–4

Builder

Practise with purpose. Find your interests.

Connect classroom learning with developing skills and academic interests. Use feedback and specialty exploration to make your next steps more deliberate.

  • Progress through skills workshops and simulation.
  • Contribute to a research task, abstract, or poster.
  • Explore relevant examination pathways and study habits.
  • Reflect on specialty exposure and potential career directions.
03

Clinical medical school · typically years 5–6

Candidate

Bring your experience together.

Develop clinical understanding and organize evidence of your work. Reflect on your experiences, strengthen your portfolio, and seek specific feedback on your preparation.

  • Keep an accurate clinical and learning log.
  • Develop research work with appropriate supervision.
  • Plan relevant examination preparation and elective applications.
  • Review your CV, personal statement, and portfolio.
04

Internship and the application period

Applicant

Make informed applications.

Translate preparation into a realistic application plan. Understand the requirements of your chosen programs, practise explaining your experience, and support students following behind you.

  • Build a program list and check official application requirements.
  • Map examination dates and application windows.
  • Prepare for interviews and request references appropriately.
  • Give back through peer teaching or mentorship.
A useful semester rhythm: one skills step, one research step, one examination or career-planning step, one mentoring conversation, and one clinical or community activity. Adapt the balance to your workload and the opportunities available.

The Surgical Passport

Give your progress
a clear record.

The Passport framework brings verified learning and contributions together, helping you reflect on your development and prepare an organized portfolio.

Joint Surgical Pathway Initiative

Surgical
Passport

Knowledge · Practice · Evidence · Reflection

More than an attendance record.

The framework records learning across the educational domains, with evidence appropriate to the activity.

  • Skills checks and tutor feedback.
  • Research tasks, presentations, and academic contributions.
  • Clinical reflection where exposure is authorized.
  • Mentoring, peer teaching, and professional development.

Verification may come from a tutor, supervisor, mentor, or activity lead. Sensitive examination or clinical information should be handled through appropriate private processes.

For Passport participation and recording arrangements, contact JSPI. This website does not collect portfolio evidence or host member accounts.

Scalpel Points

Recognize effort.
Value contribution.

Scalpel Points give structure to participation and verified development. Four recognition tiers describe accumulated progress within the JSPI framework.

0–99 points

Observer

Begin exploring the framework and identify your first learning goals.

100–249 points

First Assistant

Build consistency through learning, practice, and contribution.

250–499 points

Operator

Deepen your engagement and contribute across the domains.

500+ points

Chief

Sustain your development and give back through teaching or mentorship.

Selected activities in the points framework
Activity Points Verification
Lecture or workshop attendance 10 Activity check-in
Passing a workshop skills check +10 Tutor-scored checklist
Journal club or case presentation 15 Moderator confirmation
Mentoring conversation 5 Mentor confirmation; up to two per semester
Peer teaching 20 Participant feedback
Completed research task 15 Supervisor or team-lead confirmation
Conference abstract submission 30 Submission record

Points count after verification. Category limits keep attendance from outweighing meaningful work. Academic contributions require appropriate authorship and supervisor confirmation.

Recognition reflects activity within JSPI. It does not replace academic assessment, confer clinical privileges, or determine eligibility for residency. Access to limited-capacity activities follows the arrangements announced for each activity.
